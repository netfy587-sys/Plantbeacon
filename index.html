The Complete Beginner’s Plant Care Guide

Healthy plants are not about luck or a “green thumb”. They grow well when four basics are in balance: light, water, soil and airflow. This guide explains each one in plain language so you can care for your plants with confidence.

1. Understand Light First

Light is a plant’s food source. Most common houseplants prefer bright, indirect light, which means a spot near a window where the sun does not directly burn the leaves. Stretching stems, small pale leaves or leaning growth often suggest too little light, while brown crispy patches can suggest too much direct sun. Rotate pots every week or two so all sides get light.

2. Water Based on the Plant, Not the Calendar

Instead of watering on fixed days, check the soil: push a finger two to three centimetres into it. If it feels dry, water thoroughly until the excess drains out of the bottom. If it still feels damp, wait. Always empty saucers so roots do not sit in water.

  • Succulents and cacti: let soil dry out fully between waterings.
  • Leafy tropical plants: water when the top layer is dry.
  • Ferns and moisture lovers: keep soil lightly moist, never soggy.

3. Choose Good Soil and the Right Pot

Roots need both moisture and air. A good potting mix holds some water but drains well, and adding perlite or coarse sand helps plants that dislike wet feet. Always use a pot with drainage holes, and choose a size only slightly larger than the root ball, because an oversized pot keeps soil wet for too long.

Tip: If water runs straight through and out of the pot, the mix may be too dry or compacted. Soak the pot from below for a few minutes.

4. Know When to Repot

Roots growing out of the drainage holes, water rushing straight through, or stalled growth can be signs a plant needs a new pot. Repot in the active growing season, usually spring or early summer. Loosen the roots gently, place the plant in fresh mix and water lightly afterwards.

5. Feed Lightly

More fertiliser is not better. During spring and summer, a diluted, balanced plant fertiliser every few weeks is usually enough for many houseplants. Follow the product label and reduce or stop feeding in colder, slower months.

6. Watch for Pests and Problems

Check the undersides of leaves when you water. Sticky residue, tiny moving dots, fine webbing or sudden leaf drop deserve attention. Isolate affected plants, wipe leaves with a damp cloth and try gentle methods first. Good airflow and clean leaves prevent many problems.

7. Adjust With the Seasons

Most plants grow slower in winter. They need less water and little or no fertiliser, but may need to move closer to light as days shorten. In summer, check soil more often and protect plants from harsh afternoon sun and hot glass.

Frequently Asked Questions

Quick answers to common plant care questions.

What is PlantBeacon?

PlantBeacon is an educational website that publishes general plant care guides for homes, balconies and gardens.

How do I know if I am overwatering?

Constantly wet soil, yellowing leaves and a musty smell are common signs. Let the soil dry slightly and make sure the pot drains well.

Do all plants need direct sunlight?

No. Many houseplants prefer bright, indirect light, and direct sun can scorch some of them.

Is your advice suitable for every plant?

Our guides are general. Species and climates differ, so always check the needs of your specific plant.
